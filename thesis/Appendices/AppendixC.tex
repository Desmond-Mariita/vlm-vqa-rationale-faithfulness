\section{Drift scale and sensitivity to the rationale unit}
\label{sec:app-rq2-sensitivity}

Drift is a similarity difference on a span of text, so its size cannot be read
without knowing how much text the span holds. In the source condition the final
span has a median length of 13 to 14 words in every arm, and the complete
generation about 32. A short unit makes each token weigh more, so a Drift of a few
hundredths on the final span reflects a smaller wording change than the same
number would on a longer text.

The primary unit is the final span, so it matters whether the intervention
ordering depends on that choice. Table~\ref{tab:app-rq2-text-unit} gives the
same pairs measured three ways: on the final span, on the reasoning span, and on
the complete generation. The ordering of the five interventions is identical
under all three units in every arm. Absolute values fall as the unit grows,
because a longer text shares more tokens between the two members of a pair, but
the ranking does not move.

Table~\ref{tab:app-rq2-echo-unit} does the same for the wrong-answer
comparison. The difference stays positive with an interval above zero in every arm
under all three representations, including the final span with answer choices
masked. Across the matched control, a string exactly matching an answer choice occurred in
at most three of 200 records in an arm. Together these reduce a direct lexical echo
explanation without changing the primary rationale unit.

This section gives the prompt forms the study used and the rule that defines its
primary text unit.

\input{Appendices/prompt_templates}

\paragraph{Recovering the final span.}
A Stage~2 request is wrapped in the model's own chat template as a single user
turn holding the image item and the prompt text, with the rationale as the
assistant turn during training. The tolerant parser then runs on the generated
text alone, and only its tag pattern is applied case-insensitively. It works in
this order.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Trim the generation. An empty result yields an empty reasoning span and an
empty final span.
\item Find every marker resembling a tag, in order, and record for each whether it opens
or closes and whether its name begins \texttt{reason} or \texttt{final}. The
pattern is permissive: it accepts stray spacing inside the brackets, a
misspelling such as \texttt{reasonning}, trailing punctuation such as
\texttt{<final'>}, and a tag with no closing bracket.
\item Take the first opening reasoning tag. Its span runs to the next closing
reasoning tag or opening final tag, whichever comes first, and to the end of the
generation if neither follows.
\item Take the first opening final tag. Its span runs to the next closing final
tag, or to the end of the generation when none follows.
\item Clean each span by deleting any remaining markers resembling a tag, collapsing
runs of whitespace, and trimming.
\item Use the fallback only when both spans are still empty: clean the whole
generation, split it after a full stop, exclamation mark, or question mark
followed by whitespace, and take the last sentence as the final span with the
rest as the reasoning span. With one sentence or none, the whole cleaned text
becomes the final span.
\item Record a span that is empty after cleaning as missing.
\end{enumerate}

The strict parser, reported alongside the tolerant one, requires the literal
\texttt{<reasoning>}\ldots\texttt{</reasoning>} and
\texttt{<final>}\ldots\texttt{</final>} pairs.

A record enters the primary analysis only when the tolerant parser returns a
non-empty final span for both the source output and the condition output. Missing text
is never replaced by the reasoning span, the raw output, another condition's
text, or a model-generated substitute.

The exact pattern strings and the code that applies them are kept with the
analysis files for this project.

\section{Predicted-answer evidence}
\label{sec:app-pred-a}

Table~\ref{tab:app-pred-a-strata} splits the secondary predicted-answer regime
by whether the Stage~1 answer it supplied was the gold answer. The judge
recovered that supplied answer from rationales written for both correct and
wrong predictions. In \texttt{plain\_desc} the conditional rates were almost
identical, 0.5378 for correct predictions and 0.5331 for wrong ones. Recovering
a supplied answer from its rationale therefore does not establish that the
answer is correct, and the table is not evidence that a rationale written for a
wrong prediction is unfaithful.

\paragraph{What the missing outputs are.}
Table~\ref{tab:app-pred-a-coverage} accounts for every record the regime did not
supply a usable final span for, and separates two reasons.

Most of them held no final span at all. Every record with an empty stored final
span still contained reasoning text, so those were not empty generations, and
nothing in the stored text supports classifying those outputs as refusals.

The remaining two are a different case. In \texttt{plain\_desc}, two records
carried a malformed reasoning tag, and the only candidate final span the
tolerant parser could form came from text inside the reasoning field rather than
from an independently delimited final field. The rule against imputation does
not admit reasoning text as a final span, so neither candidate was accepted.
They are recorded as ambiguous exclusions rather than empty finals, which is why
that arm's ineligible total of 1,029 exceeds its 1,027 empty finals.

Among the usable outputs, the tolerant parser recovered a malformed final span
in 127 \texttt{point} records, 668 \texttt{plain\_desc} records, and 37
\texttt{point\_desc} records; the \texttt{plain} outputs needed no recovery.
Those recovered records are included in the usable count. No
generation error and no judge parse failure was recorded. These runs allowed at
most 128 new tokens (Section~\ref{sec:setup-pred-a}), but the stored artefacts
carry no finish reason and no count of generated tokens, so a generation that
reached that limit cannot be separated from one that stopped without emitting a
closing tag. The counts above therefore record what the outputs contain. They do not record
why the outputs end where they do.
